## Sandhi at word boundaries

First decide whether the edition applies sandhi across words. Some children's editions print words separated and unjoined; judge consistency and do not flag a missing sandhi if the whole text omits it. Where sandhi is applied, check the most frequent junctions below. Doubt anything not on this list rather than "correcting" it.

**Visarga (final `-अः`, `-आः`, `-इः`, `-उः`)**

| Final | Next word begins with | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `-अः` | `अ` | `-ओ` + `ऽ` | `सः + अपि` → `सोऽपि` |
| `-अः` | voiced consonant | `-ओ` | `रामः + गच्छति` → `रामो गच्छति` |
| `-अः` | vowel other than `अ` | visarga drops, hiatus stays | `रामः + इति` → `राम इति` |
| `-अः` | `क ख प फ` | stays `ः` | `रामः पठति` |
| `-अः` | `च` / `ट` / `त` | `श्` / `ष्` / `स्` | `रामः + च` → `रामश्च`; `रामः + तत्र` → `रामस्तत्र` |
| `-आः` | voiced consonant or any vowel | visarga drops | `बालाः + गच्छन्ति` → `बाला गच्छन्ति` |
| `-इः`, `-उः` | voiced consonant or any vowel | `-इर्`, `-उर्` | `मुनिः + अपि` → `मुनिरपि` |

**Vowels**

| Junction | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `a/ā + a/ā` | `ā` | `तत्र + अस्ति` → `तत्रास्ति` |
| `a/ā + i/ī` | `e` | `तत्र + इति` → `तत्रेति` |
| `a/ā + u/ū` | `o` | `तत्र + उवास` → `तत्रोवास` |
| `a/ā + e/ai` | `ai` | |
| `a/ā + o/au` | `au` | |
| `i/ī + other vowel` | `y` | `इति + आदि` → `इत्यादि` |
| `u/ū + other vowel` | `v` | |
| `e/o + a` | `e/o` + `ऽ` | `वने + अस्ति` → `वनेऽस्ति` |

Exceptions (no sandhi, "pragṛhya"): dual endings `-ī`, `-ū`, `-e` (`मुनी इमौ`) and the pronoun `अमी`.

**Consonants and anusvāra**

| Junction | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|
| final `म्` + consonant | `ं` | `वनम् + गच्छति` → `वनं गच्छति` |
| final `त्` + voiced | `द्` | `तत् + अपि` → `तदपि` |
| final `त्` + `च` / `श` | `च्च` / `च्छ` | `तत् + च` → `तच्च` |
| `न्` + `च` / `त` | `ंश्च` / `ंस्त` | `तान् + च` → `तांश्च` |
| `न्` after short vowel + vowel | doubles | `कुर्वन् + अपि` → `कुर्वन्नपि` |
| final `क् ट् त् प्` + voiced | `ग् ड् द् ब्` | `वाक् + दानम्` → `वाग्दानम्` |

## Case and verb forms: where things go wrong

**Nouns and pronouns**

- Stems in `-अन्` (`राजन्`, `आत्मन्`), `-ऋ` (`पितृ`, `कर्तृ`), `-इन्` (`बलिन्`), `-अत्` (`भवत्`, present participles) and feminines in `-ई` (`नदी`) are the usual casualties. Check nominative and accusative plural and the vocative singular: `राजानः`, `पितरः`, `हे राजन्`, `हे पितः`, `हे देवि`.
- Neuters: nominative = accusative; plural in `-आनि`.
- Pronouns decline irregularly: `तस्मै`, `तस्मात्`, `तस्मिन्`, `ते` (plural nominative), `एतस्मिन्`, `किम्`, `कः`, `का`. A pronoun with a plain `-आय` or `-एन` pattern (such as `ततेन`) is wrong.
- Instrumental and locative plural: `-ैः`, `-ेषु` for `-अ` stems, and see `ocr-and-orthography.md` for natva and ṣatva.

**Verbs**

- Present stems: `गच्छति`, `भवति`, `पश्यति`, `पिबति`, `तिष्ठति`, `वदति`, `करोति`, `ददाति`, `अस्ति / सन्ति`. Class errors (`गतति`, `करति`) are rare in edited text, so treat them as OCR first.
- Past narration: imperfect (`अगच्छत्`, `अभवत्`) with the augment `अ-`; perfect (`जगाम`, `उवाच`, `ऊचुः`); aorist (`अगमत्`, `अभूत्`). Missing or doubled augment is a real error: `गच्छत्` for `अगच्छत्`.
- Future: `गमिष्यति`; periphrastic `गन्ता`.
- Gerunds: `-त्वा` for a simple verb (`गत्वा`, `दृष्ट्वा`, `उक्त्वा`) and `-य` (ल्यप्) for a verb with a prefix (`आगत्य`, `प्रणम्य`, `विलोक्य`). A prefixed verb with `-त्वा` is wrong, and so is a simple verb with `-य`.
- Infinitive in `-तुम्`: `गन्तुम्`, `द्रष्टुम्`.
- Dialogue: imperative (`गच्छ`, `आगच्छ`), optative (`गच्छेत्`), passive potential (`शक्यते`).

## Agreement

- Subject and finite verb agree in person and number. A dual subject takes a dual verb.
- Adjectives, participles and demonstratives agree in gender, number and case with their noun (`सुन्दरी बालिका`, `तौ बालकौ`).
- When a text uses the pronoun `सः / सा / तत्`, the gender must match the referent, which is the usual mistake when a story is translated from a language without grammatical gender.

## Participle constructions

Past participles in `-त` / `-इत` (the `क्त` forms) are common in story narration and the main source of real grammatical error.

- With a **transitive** verb, `क्त` is passive: the agent is instrumental and the participle agrees with the object. `रामेण फलं खादितम्`. The active equivalent uses `क्तवतु`: `रामः फलं खादितवान्`.
- With an **intransitive or motion** verb, `क्त` agrees with the subject: `सा ग्रामं गता`, `राजा नगरं गतः`.
- The mixed form is wrong: `रामः फलं खादितम्` (nominative agent with a passive participle) and `रामेण फलं खादितवान्`.
- `क्त्वा` / `ल्यप्` requires the **same subject** as the main verb: `रामः ग्रामं गत्वा फलम् अखादत्`. If the subjects differ, a locative absolute (`सूर्ये अस्तं गते`) is the idiom.

## Kāraka and syntax

- Motion verbs (`गम्`, `या`, `आगम्`) take the destination in the accusative: `ग्रामं गच्छति`, not `ग्रामे गच्छति`.
- `प्रच्छ्` ("ask") takes two accusatives: `राजा मन्त्रिणं प्रश्नम् अपृच्छत्`.
- Direct speech needs `इति` after it or a verb of speaking before it. A story missing `इति` throughout is probably a translation style rather than a series of errors: report once, as a pattern.
- Correlatives pair `यः … सः` and `यदा … तदा`. A relative clause without its correlative is an error unless the sense is complete without it.
- `सह` takes the instrumental, `विना` the instrumental or ablative, `अलम्` the instrumental.
- Sanskrit word order is flexible. Do not flag order, only broken agreement.

## Compounds

- Every compound is a single word with sandhi inside and one case ending on the last member. A space inside a compound is an `OCR` or `STY` finding.
- Check the gender of the compound: it takes the gender of its final member, except for dvandvas and bahuvrīhis, which can take the gender of the thing described.
- Long compounds in children's prose are unusual. If a very long compound appears, look for a missing space that fused two words (an OCR artifact) before assuming it is intended.

## Calques from Hindi, Marathi, Telugu and English

Chandamama was published in many languages, so a Sanskrit text may be a translation. Typical signs:

- `अस्ति` used like an English "is" in every sentence, where Sanskrit would drop the copula.
- The Hindi ergative `ने` mimicked by a stray nominative agent with a `kta` participle (see above).
- Hindi `कि` rendered as `किम्` instead of `यत्` or `इति`.
- `भी` rendered as `अपि` in every clause.
- English-style definite article: `सः` or `एषः` before almost every noun.
- A Hindi or Marathi word left in the text (`लड़का`, `गाँव`): `LEX` finding with the Sanskrit equivalent (`बालकः`, `ग्रामः`).

Report a calque as one pattern finding with a few examples. Do not list every instance.

## Story conventions

- Typical openings: `कस्मिंश्चित् नगरे`, `कस्मिंश्चित् वने`, `एकदा`, `अथ`, `तदा`, `ततः`. Typical closings: `इति`, `इत्येवम्`.
- Narration mixes imperfect, perfect and `kta`/`ktavatu`. A switch of tense inside one paragraph is a `STY` note at most, never an error by itself.
- Proper names from other languages are usually Sanskritised (`विक्रमादित्यः`, `वेतालः`). Spell a name the same way each time (`CNS`).
- Verse quoted inside prose usually carries a verb of saying before it (`इति उक्तम्`, `श्लोकम् अपठत्`) or the word `यथा`. Hand it to Step 4.
